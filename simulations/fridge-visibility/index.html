Camera visibility on a moving bottle: batch study

48 random door events (shelf, lane, front-most row 1–3, 70% removals, random stock at fill 25%/50%/75%/100%, seeded), identical for every config. Flat SKOPE wire shelves (see-through), all other shelves stocked, forearm modelled. Timeline 3.6 s: door opens 1.0 s, bottle carried 1.6 s, door closes 1.0 s; sampled at 10 fps. Time-on-can = seconds the best camera sees ≥50% (or ≥25%) of the moving bottle's projected pixels, from the same ray-cast used for the camera views (walls, opaque shelves, stock and forearm occlude; 88x66 ray grid). home = visible fraction of the same bottle in its slot, door closed, no hand (what a before/after still can see). Runtime 76 s. Packs: dairy mix, as stocked, one shape per lane.

Feasibility read. The carried bottle is worth 1.6 s of camera time at most (the move phase), and a real capture pipeline on the SCS 800 / Pi class gets a handful of stills per event, not video. Configs whose time-on-can is under ~0.5 s or whose events never seen column is high cannot support take-detection from the event itself; for those, the only workable signal is before/after differencing of the settled shelf, which is bounded by the home vis column instead. Read the two columns together: high home + low time-on-can means "count, don't watch"; low home means the slot itself is invisible and no amount of frame rate helps.
