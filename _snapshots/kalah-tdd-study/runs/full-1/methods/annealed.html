How it works

Family: Step size. Idea: Uphill refactors allowed early on a fixed cooling schedule. What it changes in the search: Accepts worse designs early then cools.

throughout:
  2. "Hot may move nouns and verbs. Warm moves verbs. Cold renames and extracts."
  3. "Timebox and allowed rise: hot 45 min 20%. Warm 15 min 5%. Cold 5 min 0%."
repeat per feature until none is open:
  1. "Run classic TDD. Log energy per commit: PMD warnings plus jdeps class edges."
  4. "After each green try at most one allowed move on a throwaway branch."
  5. "Pick the move after which the next test needs the fewest changed lines to pass."
  6. "Merge if energy does not rise. Merge a rise within budget with a one-line reason."
  7. "Revert any move still red or over budget when its timebox ends."
  8. "Cool one phase per third of the original test list. Stay cold after."

Once means one agent runs the steps. Repeat per unit means one agent per unit until no unit is open. Throughout means the rule joins every prompt. For up to n starts means separate attempts with the best kept. The harness does means the harness runs the step without an agent. If names the event that starts a stage, and with adds a rule borrowed from another method. Steps are quoted from args.json with their numbers. Tag: verified.

Review scores

A blind reviewer agent scored src/ and test/ from 1, worst, to 5, best, so the scores are unchecked. Reasons are the reviewer's own words, verbatim. This review is superseded by the blind rating of three raters in rating.json, whose medians fill the comparison's principle columns.

Review scores, 11 rows and 4 columns

Alterability. Item: Change Case 1: sow seeds anti-clockwise. Score /5: 4.

Reason: Sowing walks one fixed lap and the direction is only a mirror of the printed board, so the change touches just printBoard. That method has two clockwise branches, so the variant still needs edits in two places.

Alterability. Item: Change Case 2: 7 houses with 5 seeds. Score /5: 5.

Reason: The variant is set by two ternaries for houseCount and seedCount. Every index, loop, border and capture formula comes from houseCount, so nothing else changes.

Code quality principles. Item: 1. Clear Presentation. Score /5: 4.

Reason: Formatting and indentation are consistent and the methods are short. The placement is a little loose: the static constant sits after the instance fields, and the helpers are not grouped by role (rules versus printing).

Code quality principles. Item: 2. Explanatory Language. Score /5: 4.

Reason: Names such as sow, capture, storeIndex and seedsInHouses are descriptive, and the short comments explain the lap layout. Bare idioms like 3 - player and 2 * houseCount - last have no named constant or helper.

Code quality principles. Item: 3. Consistent Code. Score /5: 4.

Reason: Players and houses are 1-based throughout and are accessed uniformly through index/storeIndex. printBoard mixes that convention with direct pits[houseCount] and pits[pits.length - 1] lookups for the stores.

Code quality principles. Item: 4. Used Content. Score /5: 5.

Reason: Every field, constant and helper is used and no dead code remains. The starter's leftover stub output was removed.

Code quality principles. Item: 5. Simple Constructs. Score /5: 4.

Reason: Control flow is a simple loop with early returns, and the methods are small. The while(true) loop with continue and the long compound capture condition take a little effort to follow.

Code quality principles. Item: 6. Minimal Duplication. Score /5: 4.

Reason: Helpers like repeat, houses, pit and store remove most repetition. printBoard still repeats the store-plus-houses row assembly in two mirrored if/else blocks.

Code quality principles. Item: 7. Modular Structure. Score /5: 3.

Reason: All game state, rules and rendering sit in one class around a raw int array, with no board or player abstraction. Functions are single-purpose, but presentation and rules are not split into modules.

Code quality principles. Item: 8. Problem Alignment. Score /5: 3.

Reason: Domain terms like sow, capture, store and house appear in the code. Players are bare ints 1 and 2, the board is an index-arithmetic array, and clockwise exists only as a display flag, which hides the domain model.

Test quality. Item: Test quality. Score /5: 3.

Reason: Eleven clearly named, independent spec-driven tests pin the core rules: empty house, extra turn, skipping the opponent store, capture, no capture, game end and tie. Almost all run only on anticlockwise 6-4, and 7-5 plus clockwise are covered mostly by quit-at-start, so the variants are pinned only thinly.

Column definitions, 12 rows and 4 columns

Family. Source: tdd-methodologies.md. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: One of four groups of methods in the Summary table of the methods doc, by what each changes in classic TDD.

Planned contrast. Source: contrast.json. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: A pair of methods and an outcome fixed before any replicate build, tested by an exact two-sided Mann-Whitney U over the replicates. U counts the replicate pairs with this method above its partner.

Holm p. Source: contrast.json. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: The planned contrast's p after Holm correction over the planned pairs. Below 0.05 separates the pair.

Adjusted mutation. Source: metrics.json. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: The mutants the build's own tests kill or time out, over those plus the survivors the hidden suite kills. Survivors the hidden suite also misses are likely equivalent and left out.

Unadjusted mutation. Source: metrics.json. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: Killed mutants over all compiled mutants.

Classes. Source: metrics.json from javap. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: Top-level, nested and anonymous classes in src/. Class diagrams show class names and relations only. A solid line ending in a hollow triangle points to a parent class and a dashed one to an implemented interface. A line with a diamond at the owning class marks a field and a plain arrow a use. Method and field totals are in the tables.

Cost to completion. Source: curve.json from costs.json with steps.csv for a method or batch.json tcp.calls for an arm. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: Weighted tokens of the lead cost plus the final clone's step costs, measure and review agents left out. A method's steps are the steps.csv rows of its graded points. An arm's steps are its loop calls, each costed by the costs.json agent its batch.json tcp.calls label names. A stopped call without a sha counts though it grades no point.

Strict PMD violations. Source: metrics.json from pmd.txt. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: Distinct PMD violations against the starter's kalah_pmd.xml. Reports alike in file, line, rule and message count once.

CQP. Source: rating.json. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: The summed median blind rating over the four cqp.html principles, 4 to 20.

CQP accepted. Source: rating.json. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: The summed median rating over the principles accepted on full-1's finals, fixed before any replicate build.

Relative spread. Source: replicate.py. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: A method's mean absolute deviation from its own median over the mean deviation of the replicated methods off the metric's bound. 1 is the average. At bound means at least 60 percent of builds sit at the bound and not comparable that fewer than two methods sit off it.

Consistency score C. Source: replicate.py. Tag: recomputed.

Definition: The mean relative spread over unadjusted mutation, classes and cost to completion. Exploratory.
